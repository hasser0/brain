## Regex languages are DFA languages

Given the regular expression $\varepsilon$ exists its the corresponding automata
with a single state that is both initial an final with no edges.

Given the regular expression $a$ exists its corresponding automata with two
different nodes, the initial and the final state and a single edge with $a$ from
the initial to the final edge

Given the regular expression $\varnothing$ exists its corresponding automata
with a single initial state with no edges and is not a final state

Suppose we are given the regular expressions $E, F$, then any regular expression
is expressed using the previous base steps and inductively applying the operands
for concatenation, addition and closure.

The concatenation $EF$ is represented by and automata which

1. Initial state of $EF$ is the initial state of $E$
2. The final state of $E$ connects to the initial state of $F$ with an epsilon
   transition
3. The final state of $F$ is the final state of $EF$

The addition $E+F$ is represented by and automata which

1. Has an initial state which connects with epsilon transitions to the initial
   states of $E$ and $F$
2. The final states of $E$ and $F$ connects with epsilon transitions to a common
   state, which is the final state of $E+F$

The closure $E^*$ is represented by an automata which

1. Has an initial and final state independent of $E$
2. The initial state of $E^*$ connects with epsilon transitions to the initial
   state of $E$ and the final state of $E^*$
3. The final state of $E$ connects to its initial state and to the final state
   of $E^*$
